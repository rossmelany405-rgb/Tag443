The Return of the Guilds

The guild system is the natural professional organisation of free societies and educated entrepreneurs.

In the late 17th century, civilised societies organised their trade through guilds. Throughout history the guild system represented society's natural impulse to govern itself against the cyclical eruptions of central power. However, the guilds suffered from their own weaknesses, which gave them an inner cycle of prosperity and decline.

In the 18th century the guilds became reputable institutions. They ranged from societies of craftsmen, brick masons and sculptors to bakers and millers, and they maintained and protected the quality and standards of their products. Societies prospered, and the guilds became influential in civil rule, in the establishment of law and order, and in spiritual growth.

The guilds did not survive the heavy pressure put on them in the 19th century. Their spirit, however, never disappeared completely. The modern university system has its origins in the guilds, and today's professional colleges are also reminiscent of them. In Germany the guilds kept their dominance and kept expanding well into the middle of the 19th century. In the rest of Europe, the growing centralised state had already reduced their influence.
